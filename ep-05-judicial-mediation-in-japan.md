In this episode, Cecep Mustafa examines Japan's civil court digitalization under the Code of Civil Procedure Amendments (Act No. 48 of 2022) and the 2023 Integration Act (Act No. 53 of 2023), with particular attention to the mints platform and the use of web conferencing in court-annexed conciliation. He compares Japan's digital judicial infrastructure with Indonesia's Electronic Court Mediation framework under Supreme Court Regulation (PERMA) No. 3 of 2022, exploring how digital platforms can support more accessible, efficient, and secure dispute resolution. The episode highlights how interoperable digital infrastructure and clear procedural safeguards can support the next generation of technology-enabled court mediation.

## Why This Topic Matters

Integrating digital technology into court-annexed mediation reduces geographic travel barriers, lowers dispute resolution costs, and speeds up document exchange. However, digitalizing dispute resolution without structured pre-trial preparation can create technical hurdles and reduce interpersonal engagement, particularly in complex commercial or sensitive family cases. Analyzing Japan's statutory civil court digitalization offers actionable technical and procedural insights for enhancing electronic mediation while safeguarding due process and procedural fairness.

## Japan Perspective

Japan's civil litigation digitalization is established under amendments to the Code of Civil Procedure (Act No. 48 of 2022) and the 2023 Integration Act (Act No. 53 of 2023). The statutory reform establishes a full digital transition structured across three technological pillars.

The first pillar is mandatory e-filing. All licensed attorneys are required to submit pleadings, petitions, and documentary evidence electronically through the Supreme Court mints platform.

The second pillar is e-case management, providing judges, legal counsel, and conciliation committees with secure digital access to digitized case records.

The third pillar is e-court proceedings, incorporating web-conferencing software (primarily Microsoft Teams) for preparatory proceedings, witness examinations, and conciliation hearings.

Digitalization directly supports Japan's civil conciliation infrastructure (Minji Chōtei). By requiring advance digital submission of pleadings and documentary evidence, Conciliation Committees (Chōtei Iinkai) conduct pre-session issue-sorting (sōten seiri). This enables panels to assess complex financial and property disputes efficiently, keeping virtual sessions focused on core valuation issues. Over the past decade, self-represented (pro se) litigation in Japanese district courts decreased from 20 percent to 7 percent as representation standardized around digital workflows.

COMPARATIVE PERSPECTIVE A comparative evaluation reveals contrasting technological architectures and operational realities between Japan and Indonesia.

Both legal systems have promulgated statutory rules to authorize online court proceedings. Indonesia enacted Supreme Court Regulation (PERMA) No. 3 of 2022 to govern Electronic Court Mediation (Mediasi di Pengadilan Secara Elektronik) within its existing e-Court portal. PERMA 3/2022 permits virtual teleconferencing upon party consent or mediator determination and authorizes electronic signatures (tanda tangan elektronik) for mediation schedules, caucus notes, and final Settlement Deeds (Akta Dading). Japan enacted Act No. 48 of 2022 to mandate digital filing via mints and authorize web-conferencing for conciliation and trial hearings.

However, operational implementation reflects key structural differences.

In Indonesia, e-mediation under PERMA 3/2022 effectively reduces travel expenses for urban and corporate litigants. However, its adoption in regional courts remains constrained by internet bandwidth disparities and a lack of dedicated virtual caucus software. Furthermore, online sessions in sensitive family disputes can limit a mediator's ability to evaluate non-verbal cues.

In Japan, digital conciliation is integrated directly into structured issue-sorting (sōten seiri). Digitized evidence is reviewed by the panel prior to online hearings, ensuring that virtual sessions address specific financial or legal impasses rather than unstructured discussions.

Observe → Understand → Compare → Adapt → Pilot → Improve

The operational methodology for adapting Japanese e-litigation practices into Indonesian court reform follows six structured stages:

Observe: Identify operational constraints in Indonesia's e-mediation under PERMA 3/2022, including internet connectivity gaps in regional courts and unintegrated virtual caucus tools.

Understand: Recognize that Japan's digital conciliation succeeds because online hearings are paired with advance digital document submission and structured issue-sorting (sōten seiri).

Compare: Contrast Indonesia's voluntary e-mediation portal against Japan's mandatory e-filing system (mints) and web-conferencing architecture under Act No. 48 of 2022.

Adapt: Formulate four technical and procedural upgrades for Mahkamah Agung under PERMA 3/2022:

Build secure digital evidence rooms (Ruang Bukti Digital) within the e-Court portal for advance document uploads prior to mediation sessions.

Integrate automated scheduling and virtual caucus breakout rooms into the e-mediation software interface.

Embed certified electronic signature validation (tanda tangan elektronik tersertifikasi) for remote signing of Settlement Deeds (Akta Dading).

Establish bandwidth allocation standards and hardware specifications for trial court virtual mediation rooms.

Pilot: Deploy the upgraded e-mediation module across four pilot courts: PN Jakarta Selatan, PA Jakarta Selatan, PN Surabaya, and PA Sleman.

Improve: Measure performance against target pilot benchmarks, aiming for at least 30 percent of pilot mediation sessions conducted online, 100 percent digital signature compliance, and a reduction in average settlement duration to 18 calendar days or fewer.

## Judicial Knowledge Takeaways

Pre-Session Document Digitization Sharpens Virtual Hearings: Requiring advance digital submission of evidence allows neutrals to review facts beforehand and conduct focused online sessions.

Integrated Virtual Caucuses Preserve Negotiation Confidentiality: Embedding secure breakout rooms into court platforms enables mediators to conduct private caucuses without leaving the primary virtual session.

Certified Electronic Signatures Ensure Settlement Validity: Incorporating verified digital signatures prevents post-settlement challenges regarding party authorization and consent.

Technical Infrastructure Must Match Procedural Mandates: Deploying online mediation rules requires standardized courtroom hardware and internet bandwidth guarantees to prevent procedural delays.

## Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What statutes govern civil court digitalization in Japan? A: Civil court digitalization in Japan is governed by amendments to the Code of Civil Procedure (Act No. 48 of 2022) and the 2023 Integration Act (Act No. 53 of 2023).

Q: What is Japan's mints platform? A: The mints platform is the official Supreme Court of Japan digital system used by licensed attorneys for mandatory electronic filing of pleadings and documentary evidence.

Q: What regulation governs electronic court mediation in Indonesia? A: Electronic court mediation in Indonesia is governed by Supreme Court Regulation (PERMA) No. 3 of 2022 on Electronic Court Mediation.

Q: How does advance digital filing improve virtual mediation? A: Advance digital filing allows conciliation committees and mediators to complete early issue-sorting (sōten seiri), ensuring virtual sessions focus on specific legal or financial disagreements.

Q: What technical upgrades are proposed for Indonesia's e-mediation portal? A: Proposed upgrades include secure digital evidence rooms, integrated virtual caucus breakout tools, automated session scheduling, and certified electronic signature validation.
